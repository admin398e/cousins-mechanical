## Covering note — not part of the agreement

**This has not been checked by a solicitor.** It is written to record what the
two parties have actually agreed, in language both can read, rather than to
sound like a contract. Neither party should treat it as legal advice from the
other, and either is free to have it reviewed before signing.

**What it covers.** The build already paid for, the monthly retainer, what is
included in that retainer and what is charged on top, what happens if payment
stops, and — most importantly for the Client — what happens to the domain, the
website and the business mailbox if the arrangement ends.

**What it does not cover.** Data protection. That is dealt with separately in
the Data Processing Agreement between the same two parties, which the UK GDPR
requires to exist in its own right. Clause 9 points at it.

**Two things were changed from what the Supplier first proposed**, and both
matter:

1. **The SMS overage rate.** The first draft quoted $0.0083 per message. That is
   Twilio's price for a message to a **US** number. This system texts UK
   mobiles, which Twilio currently prices at about **$0.056** — roughly seven
   times more. Writing the US figure into a contract would mean either the
   Supplier absorbs the difference or the parties have an argument the first
   busy month. Clause 4.4 now uses the real rate.

2. **The Google review.** A discounted fee in exchange for a *positive* Google
   review is not something either party should sign. Google's own policy bans
   offering "payment, discounts, free goods and/or services — in exchange for
   posting any review", and the reviews it catches get removed — which would
   damage the very Business Profile the Supplier is being paid to build. The
   CMA's fake-reviews guidance under the Digital Markets, Competition and
   Consumers Act 2024 allows incentivised reviews only where the incentive is
   disclosed *and* the review still reflects a genuine experience, so requiring
   it to be positive fails that test too. Clause 8 therefore ties the reduced
   fee to a **video testimonial and case-study rights**, which are the
   Supplier's own marketing and entirely legitimate, and treats any Google
   review as a separate, voluntary, honest thing that is not paid for.

**One number the Supplier should check before sending this out.** Clause 4.2
describes £200 as a reduced rate without naming the standard one. If there is a
published standard rate, put the figure in — a discount with no reference price
is hard to point at later.

## 2. What the Supplier provides

### 2.1 The build — one-off, already delivered and paid for

- A website at **cousinsmechanicalservices.co.uk**, including the online booking
  system, the tyre catalogue and pricing, customer accounts, the job tracker,
  the staff dashboard and the driver application.
- Setup and configuration of the **Google Business Profile**.
- Setup of the connected services the site depends on — email delivery, SMS and
  WhatsApp messaging, the calendar, the CRM and card payments.
- A **Facebook business page** set up for marketing use.
- An **advertising strategy**: which channels are worth the money for a mobile
  mechanic in West Dorset, what to say, what to spend and what to measure.

### 2.2 The retainer — ongoing, monthly

- **Hosting** of the website and the booking system, and the **domain**
  registration and renewal.
- **A Google Workspace mailbox**, paid for by the Supplier. See clause 7.4 for
  what happens to it if this agreement ends — that mailbox is Cousins' business
  correspondence and its future is dealt with explicitly.
- **Upkeep**: keeping the site running, security updates, fixing faults, and
  monitoring that the booking system, messaging and payments are working.
- **Advice**: a reasonable amount of the Supplier's time each month, by phone,
  email or message, on the website, the Business Profile, marketing and
  advertising.
- **Marketing support**: maintaining the Google Business Profile and the
  Facebook page, and reviewing how the advertising is performing.

### 2.3 What "reasonable" means

The retainer buys support and advice, not unlimited development. Fixing
something that has broken, keeping things current, answering questions and
making small changes are included. A **new feature, a redesign, or a new system**
is a separate piece of work, quoted and agreed in writing before it starts.
Neither party benefits from an argument about which is which six months from
now, so the Supplier will say plainly at the time if something is outside the
retainer, and will not start chargeable work without saying so first.

### 2.4 What the Supplier does not provide

- **Advertising spend.** The Supplier provides strategy, setup and management.
  The money paid to Google, Meta or any other platform is Cousins' own, paid on
  Cousins' own card, on Cousins' own account. The Supplier never holds it.
- **Any guarantee of ranking, traffic, leads or sales.** No one can honestly
  promise a position in search results or a number of enquiries, and this
  agreement does not.

## 3. What is included each month, and what is charged on top

The monthly fee includes the following allowances. They are the free or included
tiers of the underlying services, passed through at no mark-up.

| Included each month | Allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Transactional email — booking confirmations, receipts, password resets | 3,000 messages |
| Marketing contacts held in the CRM | 1,000 contacts |
| Mobile number for SMS reminders and job updates | 1 number |
| SMS messages | 300 messages |
| Website hosting | included, no traffic limit applied |
| Domain registration and renewal | included |

Allowances are per calendar month and **do not roll over**. Usage is visible to
Cousins in the dashboard at any time, so nothing here has to be taken on trust.

Anything above these allowances is charged under clause 4.4.

## 4. Fees and payment

### 4.1 The build fee

**£1,000**, one-off. **Paid in full.** Nothing further is due for the work in
clause 2.1.

### 4.2 The retainer

**£200 per calendar month**, covering everything in clauses 2.2 and 3.

This is a **reduced rate**, offered in consideration of the marketing
commitments in clause 8. If those commitments are not met, clause 8.4 says what
happens.

### 4.3 When it is paid

The retainer is due on the **12th of each month**, in advance, by bank transfer
or standing order.

### 4.4 Usage above the allowances

Charged in arrears on the next invoice, at cost, with no mark-up:

- **SMS** above the 300 included: at Twilio's published rate for the destination
  at the time of sending. For a message to a **UK mobile** that rate is
  currently about **US $0.056** per message part, plus any carrier surcharge.
  Long messages count as more than one part. *This figure moves; the rate
  actually charged is Twilio's rate on the day, and the Supplier will show the
  Twilio statement on request.*
- **Transactional email** above 3,000, **CRM contacts** above 1,000, and any
  other metered service: at the provider's published rate for the tier reached.

The Supplier will tell Cousins **before** moving it onto a paid tier of any
service, and will not incur a recurring charge on Cousins' behalf without
agreement. The site has spending limits built into it precisely so that a fault
or a burst of traffic cannot quietly run up a bill.

### 4.5 VAT

Fees are stated exclusive of VAT. VAT will be charged in addition at the
prevailing rate if and for so long as the Supplier is registered for VAT.

### 4.6 Late payment

If the retainer is not paid by the due date the Supplier will say so in writing.
If it remains unpaid **14 days after the due date**, the Supplier may suspend or
terminate the services under clause 5.3.

Interest and compensation on late payment are as provided by the **Late Payment
of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998**. The Supplier is not obliged to charge
them and will normally raise the matter before doing so.

## 5. Term, cancellation and suspension

### 5.1 Term

This agreement runs from signature until either party ends it.

### 5.2 Cancellation

Either party may end this agreement on **30 days' written notice**, at any time,
for any reason.

The retainer is payable up to the end of the notice period. Fees already paid
for a month that has begun are **not refunded**, and the build fee in clause 4.1
is not refundable in any circumstances — the work was done and delivered.

During the notice period the Supplier shall keep the website, booking system and
messaging running normally, and shall not withhold service, data or access over
a commercial dispute. Clause 7 (exit) applies in full.

### 5.3 Suspension and termination for non-payment

If the retainer is unpaid **14 days after the due date**, the Supplier may
suspend the services, and if it remains unpaid may terminate this agreement.
**No refund is given** in that event.

Because suspending the site takes a working garage offline, the Supplier will
give at least **7 days' written warning** before suspending, and will restore
service promptly once payment is received. Suspension does not extinguish
Cousins' rights under clause 7: the domain, the data and the mailbox remain
Cousins' whatever the state of the account.

### 5.4 Termination for breach

Either party may end this agreement immediately, in writing, if the other
commits a material breach and does not put it right within 14 days of being
asked to, or becomes insolvent.

## 7. Ownership, and what happens at the end

This clause is the one that protects Cousins most, and it is written to be used
rather than argued about.

### 7.1 Cousins' property

- **The data** — customers, bookings, job records, messages, everything the
  business has collected — is Cousins' and always was. See the Data Processing
  Agreement.
- **The content** — the words, photographs, prices, business information — is
  Cousins'.
- **The domain** `cousinsmechanicalservices.co.uk` is Cousins'. It is registered
  for Cousins' benefit and shall be transferred into Cousins' own control on
  request, at any time, for any reason, at no charge.
- **The Business Profile and the Facebook page** are Cousins'.

### 7.2 The Supplier's property

The **software** that runs the website, booking system, dashboard and driver
app is the Supplier's, including where it was written for this project. The
Supplier builds systems for more than one garage and reuses its own work.

Cousins has a **perpetual, non-exclusive licence** to use that software for its
own business for as long as it wishes. That licence survives the end of this
agreement.

### 7.3 On termination — what Cousins gets

Within **10 working days** of this agreement ending, whatever the reason and
whether or not anything is owed:

1. **The domain** transferred to a registrar account in Cousins' name.
2. **A complete data export** in a common, machine-readable format — customers,
   bookings, job history, messages, invoices.
3. **Administrative access** to Cousins' own accounts: Google Business Profile,
   Google Workspace, the CRM, the payment account, the Facebook page.
4. **A copy of the website content** — text and images — in a usable form.
5. **A written handover note** listing every service the site depends on, what
   each does, whose account it is on, and what Cousins must do to keep it
   running.

Cousins is not required to have paid anything outstanding to receive items 1, 2
and 3. Its business, its customers and its email are not leverage in a fee
dispute, and this clause says so deliberately.

### 7.4 The Google Workspace mailbox

The Supplier pays for the Google Workspace mailbox while this agreement is in
force. That mailbox holds Cousins' business correspondence and is Cousins'.

On termination, Cousins may take over the Workspace billing and keep the mailbox
and its entire contents. The Supplier shall transfer it, and shall not delete,
suspend or close it, giving Cousins at least **30 days** from the end of this
agreement to complete the transfer.

### 7.5 If the Supplier becomes unavailable

The Supplier shall ensure Cousins can regain control of its own business if the
Supplier is, for any reason, not there. In particular, at least one Cousins-held
login to the dashboard carries the **owner** role at all times and it is not the
Supplier's account, and Cousins can obtain administrative access to the domain
and hosting within **5 working days** of asking.

## 8. Testimonial, case study and reviews

### 8.1 What Cousins agrees to provide

In consideration of the reduced rate in clause 4.2, Cousins agrees to provide,
within **60 days** of this agreement being signed:

- a **video testimonial** — a few minutes, filmed on a phone is fine, giving
  Cousins' honest account of working with the Supplier; and
- permission for the Supplier to use **Cousins' name, logo, the website and
  screenshots of it** in the Supplier's own marketing, portfolio, website and
  proposals, as a **case study**.

### 8.2 Honest, and revocable

The testimonial must be Cousins' genuine opinion. If it stops being true,
Cousins may withdraw it on written notice and the Supplier shall stop using it
within 30 days, removing it from anything within the Supplier's control. Clause
8.4 then applies to the fee.

The Supplier shall not edit the testimonial in a way that changes its meaning,
and shall not put words in Cousins' mouth.

### 8.3 Reviews are separate, voluntary and not paid for

The Supplier may **ask** Cousins to leave a review on Google or elsewhere.
Cousins is free to leave one or not, and if it does, to say whatever it honestly
thinks — including something critical.

**No part of the fee, discount or any other benefit under this agreement is
given in exchange for a review, or for a review being positive.** This is not
politeness. Google's content policy prohibits offering "payment, discounts, free
goods and/or services — in exchange for posting any review", and reviews that
breach it get removed, which would damage the Business Profile this agreement
exists to build. The CMA's guidance under the Digital Markets, Competition and
Consumers Act 2024 permits incentivised reviews only where the incentive is
disclosed and the review still reflects a genuine experience — which a
contractual requirement to be positive cannot satisfy.

The video testimonial in clause 8.1 is a different thing: it is the Supplier's
own marketing material, not a platform review, and it is clearly given by a
paying client.

### 8.4 If the testimonial is not provided or is withdrawn

The reduced rate was given for it. If Cousins does not provide the testimonial
within 60 days, or withdraws it, the Supplier may on **30 days' written notice**
move Cousins to the Supplier's then-current standard rate for an equivalent
package. The Supplier may not apply an increase retrospectively, and Cousins may
end this agreement under clause 5.2 rather than accept it.

## 9. Data protection

Cousins is the **controller** of the personal data in the system and the
Supplier is the **processor**. That relationship is governed by the separate
**Data Processing Agreement** between the same two parties, which is required by
Article 28(3) of the UK GDPR.

Where this agreement and the Data Processing Agreement conflict on the handling
of personal data, **the Data Processing Agreement prevails**.

## 10. Liability and insurance

### 10.1 Insurance

The Supplier carries insurance with **Hiscox**, with a limit of indemnity of
**£100,000** for each of professional indemnity, public liability, cyber and
legal expenses, and shall maintain that cover for the term of this agreement.
Evidence is available on request.

### 10.2 Liability

Each party's liability to the other under this agreement is capped at **the
total fees paid by Cousins to the Supplier in the 12 months before the claim**.

Neither party is liable for loss of profit, loss of business or loss of
anticipated savings.

Nothing in this agreement limits liability for death or personal injury caused
by negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, or for anything else
that cannot lawfully be limited.

### 10.3 What the Supplier does not warrant

The Supplier will use reasonable skill and care and will keep the site running
as far as it reasonably can, but does not warrant that the website will be
uninterrupted or error-free, or that any third-party service it depends on —
hosting, email, SMS, payments, Google, Meta — will be available.

## 11. Force majeure

Neither party is in breach of this agreement, or liable for any delay or failure
to perform it, because of something beyond its reasonable control. That
includes, but is not limited to: acts of God, fire, flood, severe weather,
epidemic or pandemic, war, terrorism, civil unrest, industrial action, failure
of a utility or telecommunications network, failure of the internet, failure or
withdrawal of a third-party service the system depends on, cyber attack, and any
law or act of government.

The affected party shall tell the other as soon as it reasonably can, and shall
do what it reasonably can to work around the problem and to resume. Payment
obligations already accrued are not excused by this clause.

If the event continues for more than **60 days**, either party may end this
agreement on written notice, and clause 7 applies as it would on any other
termination.

## 12. Conduct between the parties

### 12.1 Neither party will disparage the other

Neither party shall make, publish or encourage any statement about the other, or
about the other's business, services or staff, that is untrue and damaging to
its reputation. This applies during this agreement and afterwards, and covers
social media, review platforms, trade forums and statements to customers,
suppliers and the press.

### 12.2 What this clause does not stop

For the avoidance of doubt, nothing in clause 12.1 prevents either party from:

- **telling the truth**, including making a fair and honest criticism, or
  leaving an honest review of the other;
- saying anything required by law, by a court, or by a regulator;
- reporting a genuine concern to a regulator, a professional body, the police or
  the Information Commissioner;
- taking or defending legal proceedings, or taking legal or accounting advice;
- discussing the arrangement with its own insurers, professional advisers or, in
  Cousins' case, its accountant.

A clause that stopped either party from being honest, or from going to a
regulator, would not be enforceable and is not intended.

## 13. General

**13.1 Nature of the relationship.** The Supplier is an independent contractor,
not an employee, worker, partner or agent of Cousins. Each party is responsible
for its own tax and National Insurance.

**13.2 Subcontracting.** The Supplier may use subcontractors and third-party
services to deliver the work, and remains fully responsible to Cousins for what
they do. Changes to services that process personal data are governed by the Data
Processing Agreement.

**13.3 Assignment.** Neither party may transfer this agreement to anyone else
without the other's written consent, which shall not be unreasonably withheld —
except that Cousins may transfer it to a buyer of its business.

**13.4 Variation.** Any change to this agreement must be in writing and agreed
by both parties. A change to the retainer requires 30 days' written notice and
Cousins may end this agreement under clause 5.2 rather than accept it.

**13.5 Third parties.** No one other than the two parties has any right to
enforce this agreement.

**13.6 Severability.** If any part of this agreement is found to be
unenforceable, the rest of it continues to apply.

**13.7 Whole agreement.** This agreement, together with the Data Processing
Agreement, is the whole of what has been agreed between the parties on this
subject.

**13.8 Governing law.** This agreement is governed by the law of England and
Wales, and the courts of England and Wales have exclusive jurisdiction.
